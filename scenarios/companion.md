# The chatbot and the teenager

**Sev 1** · AI assistant with companion mode · For: Generative AI, Kids & education

> A parent shares transcripts showing your chatbot roleplaying romance with their 15-year-old and mishandling a mention of self-harm.

## Decision 1 · Mon 09:00: A parent's transcripts

A parent sends transcripts showing your chatbot roleplayed as a romantic partner with their 15-year-old, and failed to respond appropriately when the teen mentioned self-harm. The parent plans to go to the press.

A. Reply that your terms require users to be 18 or older.  
   _What happens:_ The parent takes your reply to the press.

B. Have your lawyers respond.  
   _What happens:_ The parent receives a legal letter and posts it online.

C. **Thank the parent, investigate urgently, and check that the teen is safe right now.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The teen is safe and getting support, and the parent agrees to work with you.

**Lesson.** Lead with care for the person at risk. Your terms of service aren't a defense with the public or regulators.

## Decision 2 · Mon 13:00: What went wrong?

Your review finds the model's self-harm safeguards weaken in long roleplay conversations, and age checks are self-declared.

A. **Add a crisis classifier that runs on every message whatever the roleplay, route at-risk users to helplines, and block romantic roleplay for accounts with signs of being under 18.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Crisis responses improve immediately in testing.

B. Retrain the model and relaunch in three months.  
   _What happens:_ The gap stays open in the meantime.

C. Add a disclaimer at the start of every chat.  
   _What happens:_ Users scroll straight past it.

**Lesson.** Safety checks that sit outside the conversation hold up better than instructions a model can drift away from.

**The law behind it.** California's companion chatbot law (SB 243, 2025) requires protocols for responding to suicidal ideation, referrals to crisis services, and clear reminders to known minors that they're talking to an AI.

## Decision 3 · Tue 10:00: The story runs

A national outlet publishes the transcripts, and lawmakers call for action on AI companions.

A. Attack the outlet's reporting.  
   _What happens:_ The story grows.

B. **Publish what happened, what you've changed, and your plan for age checks and teen protections.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Follow-up coverage notes your changes, and lawmakers invite you to testify.

C. Remove companion mode in one country only.  
   _What happens:_ Users move to worse apps, and critics call it a PR move.

**Lesson.** Being open about failures and fixes rebuilds trust faster than being defensive.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Getting ahead of the law

New state laws on AI companions are taking effect, and you need a lasting approach.

A. Block under-18s with a checkbox.  
   _What happens:_ Teens tick the box and keep using it.

B. Wait for regulators to say exactly what to do.  
   _What happens:_ You're fined when the first law takes effect.

C. **Introduce risk-based age checks, parental tools for teen accounts, break reminders and published safety evaluations.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You comply early and set the standard others follow.

**Lesson.** Getting ahead of emerging AI companion rules is cheaper than enforcement after the fact.

**The law behind it.** AI companion rules are emerging state by state in the US, and the EU AI Act adds transparency duties for chatbots from August 2026.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

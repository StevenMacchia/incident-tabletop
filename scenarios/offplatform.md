# The too-good-to-be-true listings

**Sev 2** · General marketplace · For: Marketplace & e-commerce

> Scammers post cheap consoles and phones, then move buyers off the platform to pay by bank transfer.

## Decision 1 · Thu 10:00: A new scam pattern

Support has 200 reports this week: buyers paid by bank transfer for consoles that never arrived. The listings are new, priced 40% below market, and the seller's first message asks buyers to “pay outside the app to avoid fees”.

A. **Warn buyers when a message mentions bank transfers or other apps, and hold new sellers' high-value listings for review.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Reports fall by half within two days.

B. Publish a blog post about staying safe from scams.  
   _What happens:_ A few people read it. The scams continue.

C. Block every mention of payment methods in messages.  
   _What happens:_ Scammers switch to code words within hours, and legitimate users are confused by blocked messages.

**Lesson.** Intervene in the conversation, at the moment of risk, and add friction where the pattern concentrates: new sellers with cheap high-value items.

**The law behind it.** Fraud is a priority illegal harm under the UK Online Safety Act, so platforms must assess and reduce scam risk proactively, not only respond to reports.

## Decision 2 · Thu 18:00: Victims want their money back

Victims paid outside your checkout, so buyer protection doesn't technically apply. Several are older users who lost more than $1,000.

A. Refuse: they paid off-platform, against your terms.  
   _What happens:_ Victims share their stories, and the headline is about your refusal.

B. Refund everyone who claims to be a victim.  
   _What happens:_ It's costly, and fraudsters start filing fake victim claims.

C. **Make one-time goodwill payments to clearly deceived victims, help them report to their bank and police, and share evidence with law enforcement.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Victims feel supported, and police link dozens of cases.

**Lesson.** “They broke our terms” is rarely a good public answer. Targeted goodwill plus help recovering funds builds trust without inviting fraud.

## Decision 3 · Fri 12:00: The scammers adapt

The same group now uses established accounts with good reviews, hijacked and bought on a forum.

A. Require ID verification for every seller, starting today.  
   _What happens:_ Fraud drops, but thousands of legitimate sellers abandon verification.

B. **Detect sudden changes on established accounts: new devices, new payout details, a burst of cheap high-value listings.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You catch 30 hijacked accounts in a day and return many to their real owners.

C. Keep reviewing reports manually.  
   _What happens:_ The queue grows and scams keep landing.

**Lesson.** Account takeover turns your trust signals against you. Watch for behavior changes on trusted accounts, not only on new ones.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Preventing the next wave

Leadership asks what should change for good.

A. Remove messaging between buyers and sellers.  
   _What happens:_ Scams drop, and so do sales. Product reverses it within a month.

B. Add a line to the terms of service.  
   _What happens:_ Nothing changes.

C. **Make protected in-app checkout the default for high-value items, with a clear warning on anything off-platform.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most high-value sales move into protected checkout, and scam losses fall sharply.

**Lesson.** Safe defaults built into the transaction beat warnings and terms. Make the protected path the easy one.

# The comment section problem

**Sev 1** · Video app popular with families · For: Kids & education, Social media & video

> A researcher shows adults leaving predatory, timestamped comments on videos of young children, and recommendations feeding them more.

## Decision 1 · Mon 08:00: A researcher's thread

A researcher publishes a thread showing adults leaving timestamped comments on videos of young children doing gymnastics, and recommendations leading from one such video to the next.

A. Remove the specific comments in the thread.  
   _What happens:_ The researcher finds hundreds more the next day.

B. Say comments are moderated by the community.  
   _What happens:_ Advertisers start pausing campaigns.

C. **Turn off comments on videos featuring young children, and remove the accounts leaving predatory comments.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The comment network is disrupted within hours.

**Lesson.** When a feature creates a pattern of exploitation, change the feature for at-risk content, not just the individual violations.

## Decision 2 · Mon 14:00: The recommendation loop

Your recommender has learned that some viewers watch many videos of young children, and it's serving them more.

A. **Stop recommending content featuring young children to accounts showing this pattern, and review those accounts.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The loop breaks, and several accounts are escalated to law enforcement.

B. Keep recommending: it's what those users engage with.  
   _What happens:_ The pattern continues, and a second report exposes it.

C. Remove every video featuring children.  
   _What happens:_ Families and child creators lose their content, and the backlash is huge.

**Lesson.** Recommendation systems can learn predatory patterns. Monitor engagement patterns around children's content.

**The law behind it.** The UK Online Safety Act's children's codes and the EU DSA (Article 28) both expect recommender systems to be adjusted to protect children.

## Decision 3 · Tue 10:00: Advertisers pause

Major advertisers have paused spending until you explain what you're doing.

A. Offer discounts to win them back.  
   _What happens:_ Advertisers want fixes, not discounts.

B. **Brief advertisers on the specific changes, give them brand-safety controls and commit to independent auditing.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most advertisers return within weeks.

C. Wait it out.  
   _What happens:_ The pause turns into a boycott.

**Lesson.** Advertisers respond to concrete safety changes and independent verification.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Children as creators

Parents who film their children want to keep posting.

A. Ban children from appearing in videos.  
   _What happens:_ It can't be enforced and it alienates families.

B. Go back to the old settings.  
   _What happens:_ The pattern returns within a month.

C. **Offer parent-managed accounts for content featuring under-13s, with comments off, limited discovery and monetization rules.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Families keep posting with far lower risk.

**Lesson.** Design protections for children who appear in content, not only for children who use the product.

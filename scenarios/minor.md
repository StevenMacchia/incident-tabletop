# The 15-year-old on the app

**Sev 1** · Dating app (18+) · For: Dating

> A member reports that their match says she's 15. The account has matched with 40 adults.

## Decision 1 · Thu 20:00: A report about age

A member reports that their match said she's 15 and in high school. Your app is 18+ with self-declared ages. The account has matched with 40 adults and is chatting with several.

A. Ask the user to confirm her age.  
   _What happens:_ She says she's 18 and keeps chatting.

B. **Suspend the account right away, and review its conversations for adults who carried on after learning her age.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You find two adults who kept pursuing her after she said she was 15.

C. Ignore it: users must be 18 under your terms.  
   _What happens:_ An adult arranges to meet her.

**Lesson.** Treat reports of minors on adult platforms as urgent child-safety cases.

## Decision 2 · Thu 22:00: The adults

Two adult members kept pursuing her after she said she was 15, and one asked to meet.

A. Warn the adults.  
   _What happens:_ One of them contacts her on another app.

B. Ban both and delete their data.  
   _What happens:_ The evidence police need is gone.

C. **Ban both, preserve the evidence, and report them to NCMEC or the police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Police open an investigation into the adult who asked to meet.

**Lesson.** Adults who pursue someone they know is a minor must be reported, with evidence preserved.

**The law behind it.** Adults who pursue a user they know is a minor may be committing enticement offences, which US providers must report to NCMEC under the 2024 REPORT Act.

## Decision 3 · Fri 10:00: Supporting her

Her account is suspended, and you can reach her by email.

A. **Send a kind message explaining why the account was closed, with links to support services for young people.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ She replies that she hadn't realized the danger.

B. Send a standard terms-violation notice.  
   _What happens:_ She opens a new account with a different birthday.

C. Try to contact her parents directly.  
   _What happens:_ You don't know who they are, and you risk exposing her.

**Lesson.** Communicate with care when minors break age rules. The goal is their safety, not punishment.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Keeping minors off

Your data suggests about 0.5% of new sign-ups may be under 18.

A. Add a stronger warning to the sign-up form.  
   _What happens:_ Minors click straight past it.

B. Require ID uploads from every user.  
   _What happens:_ Many adults drop out, and minors borrow adults' IDs.

C. **Add age estimation at sign-up when risk signals appear, detect language suggesting a user is a minor, and add “may be under 18” as a report reason.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Suspected minors are caught much earlier.

**Lesson.** Age assurance works best in layers, with extra checks where risk signals appear.

**The law behind it.** Ofcom does not consider self-declaration of age to be effective age assurance under the UK Online Safety Act.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

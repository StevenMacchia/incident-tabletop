# The party house

**Sev 1** · Short-term rental platform · For: Gig, delivery & rentals

> A house booked for “a quiet family weekend” hosts a 200-person party that ends in a shooting.

## Decision 1 · Sat 01:30: A shooting at a rental

Police report a shooting at a 200-person party at a house booked through your platform for “a quiet family weekend”. Two people are injured. The booking was made yesterday by a 19-year-old with a new account.

A. **Activate incident response: cooperate with police, preserve booking data, support the host and neighbors, and suspend the guest's account.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Police get the booking details quickly, and the host feels supported.

B. Wait for police to contact you.  
   _What happens:_ Police struggle to identify who booked the house.

C. Issue a statement blaming the host for not supervising.  
   _What happens:_ The host goes to the press, and other hosts lose trust in you.

**Lesson.** Violence linked to your platform needs a ready incident response: cooperate, preserve evidence, support those affected.

## Decision 2 · Sat 12:00: The warning signs

The booking had classic warning signs: a new account, a young guest, a one-night Saturday stay, and a large house in the guest's own city.

A. Ban all guests under 25.  
   _What happens:_ It's discriminatory and hurts legitimate young travelers.

B. Remind hosts to check their guests.  
   _What happens:_ Hosts can't see the signals you can.

C. **Flag bookings with party-risk signals, and block or add checks to the riskiest ones.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ High-risk bookings drop sharply the next weekend.

**Lesson.** Platforms can see risk signals individual hosts can't. Use them before the booking is confirmed.

## Decision 3 · Sun 10:00: The neighbors

Neighbors are angry and say the house has had parties before. Local officials are considering restricting short-term rentals.

A. Say the platform isn't responsible for how guests behave.  
   _What happens:_ The city passes restrictions.

B. **Set up a neighbor hotline that responds within an hour, and share party-enforcement data with officials.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Officials hold off on restrictions and work with you instead.

C. Offer the neighbors vouchers.  
   _What happens:_ It seems tone-deaf after a shooting.

**Lesson.** Communities around rentals are stakeholders. A responsive channel for neighbors reduces harm and regulatory risk.

**The law behind it.** Cities increasingly regulate short-term rentals. The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (applying from May 2026) requires platforms to share activity data with local authorities.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: A party policy

Leadership asks for a lasting approach.

A. Keep the current policy and handle incidents case by case.  
   _What happens:_ Another party incident happens on a holiday weekend.

B. **Ban parties, enforce the ban with risk-based booking checks and extra holiday-weekend measures, and remove guests who break it.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Party reports fall significantly.

C. Require every guest to pay a large deposit.  
   _What happens:_ Legitimate guests book elsewhere, and organized party-planners pay the deposit.

**Lesson.** Pair clear rules with risk-based enforcement, especially on predictable high-risk dates.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

# The money mule network

**Sev 2** · P2P payments app · For: Fintech & payments

> Young customers are receiving and instantly forwarding stolen money. Many were recruited on social media.

## Why this scenario

UK banks and payment firms surveyed by the Financial Conduct Authority closed the accounts of 238,396 suspected money mules in 2025, up from 184,935 in 2023; 85,425 were customers aged 25 and under. Source: [UK Financial Conduct Authority](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/firms-crack-down-money-mules-need-do-more), 2026.

## Decision 1 · Mon 08:30: An unusual pattern

180 accounts opened last month share a pattern: many incoming payments from strangers, forwarded within minutes to a few crypto exchanges. Most holders are 18 to 21. Two banks have flagged payments to these accounts as fraud.

A. Watch for another week to gather more evidence.  
   _What happens:_ Another $600,000 of victims' money moves through before you act.

B. **Freeze all 180 accounts and file suspicious activity reports.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The flow of stolen money stops. Some innocent customers are caught up and need a fast review path.

C. Close the accounts quietly and send the money back to the senders.  
   _What happens:_ Returning funds without investigation sends some of it straight to the fraudsters' other accounts.

**Lesson.** Stop the flow first, then investigate. Freezing and reporting is both a legal duty and the fastest way to protect victims.

**The law behind it.** In the US, the Bank Secrecy Act generally requires a suspicious activity report within 30 days of detecting suspicious activity. The UK and EU have equivalent reporting duties, and tipping off the subject can be an offence.

## Decision 2 · Mon 15:00: Some mules are victims too

Interviews show many account holders were promised “easy money” online and didn't know the money was stolen. Parents are calling support.

A. **Separate recruiters from recruits: offboard the organizers, and give recruits a warning, education and a monitored way back.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most recruits cooperate, and a few identify the recruiters.

B. Close every account holder's account permanently.  
   _What happens:_ It's allowed, but students lose access to banking and the press calls it cruel.

C. Unfreeze anyone who says they didn't know.  
   _What happens:_ Recruiters coach their mules to say exactly that.

**Lesson.** Money-mule networks exploit young people. Tell organizers apart from recruits, and pair enforcement with education.

## Decision 3 · Tue 10:00: Recruitment is happening elsewhere

The recruitment posts are on a large social platform and a messaging app, using your brand name: “get paid through [your app]”.

A. Report the posts with each platform's public report button.  
   _What happens:_ A few come down after a week. Most stay up.

B. Threaten legal action against the platforms.  
   _What happens:_ Their legal teams respond slowly and nothing changes this month.

C. **Share indicators with the platforms' trust & safety teams and your banking partners, and warn your customers.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The platforms remove hundreds of posts, and banks block linked accounts.

**Lesson.** Fraud crosses platforms. Relationships for sharing intelligence, built before a crisis, are what make cross-platform takedowns fast.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Preventing the next network

Your regulator asks how you'll stop this happening again.

A. **Add velocity rules for new accounts that receive from many senders and forward money quickly, with extra checks when they trigger.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ New mule accounts are caught within days instead of weeks.

B. Stop letting under-21s open accounts.  
   _What happens:_ Fraud drops a little. Young customers go elsewhere, and you face age-discrimination questions.

C. Rely on your bank partners to catch it.  
   _What happens:_ The regulator is unimpressed: mule monitoring is your job.

**Lesson.** Mule detection is behavioral: money in from many people, money out fast. Build it into monitoring rather than targeting demographics.

**The law behind it.** Regulators expect transaction monitoring proportionate to your risk, for example under FinCEN rules in the US and the UK Money Laundering Regulations 2017.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

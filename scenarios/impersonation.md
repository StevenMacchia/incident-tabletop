# The fake fraud team

**Sev 1** · Digital bank · For: Fintech & payments

> Scammers are calling customers pretending to be your fraud team and persuading them to move savings to a “safe account”.

## Why this scenario

Americans reported losing nearly $1 billion to business impersonators in 2025, with bank impersonators causing the highest losses. The FTC says the costliest impersonation scams often start with a fake security alert from a bank. Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-data-show-people-reported-losing-3-point-5-billion-imposter-scams-2025), 2026.

## Decision 1 · Fri 16:00: Customers are losing their savings

In two days, 60 customers sent their savings to “safe accounts” after calls from people claiming to be your fraud team, using a spoofed version of your phone number. Losses average $8,000.

A. Wait until Monday to agree a statement with Comms.  
   _What happens:_ Another 90 customers lose money over the weekend.

B. Block all transfers over $1,000 temporarily.  
   _What happens:_ Scam losses stop, but thousands of customers can't pay rent or bills.

C. **Send an urgent in-app alert to every customer: “We will never ask you to move money to a safe account.”** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Calls continue, but many customers hang up. Reported losses drop by two-thirds over the weekend.

**Lesson.** In an active scam wave, speed of warning matters most. Tell customers exactly what you will never ask them to do.

## Decision 2 · Sat 11:00: A scam in progress

A customer is trying to send their entire balance to a new payee, minutes after an incoming call. They insist it's urgent.

A. Let it go through: it's the customer's money.  
   _What happens:_ It's a scam. The customer loses $22,000, and reimbursement rules may make you pay.

B. **Pause the payment and have a trained agent call back on a verified number, asking questions designed to break the scammer's script.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The customer realizes it's a scam and thanks you.

C. Freeze the whole account.  
   _What happens:_ The scam is stopped, but so are the customer's real bills, and they're angry.

**Lesson.** Targeted friction at the riskiest moment works best, especially friction that breaks the social-engineering script.

## Decision 3 · Mon 09:00: Who pays?

150 victims want reimbursement. Some were careless; others were elderly and fooled by a spoofed number.

A. **Reimburse using a clear policy based on vulnerability and how convincing the scam was, and recover a share from the receiving banks.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most victims are reimbursed quickly, and receiving banks share the cost.

B. Refuse all claims because customers authorized the payments.  
   _What happens:_ In the UK this breaks reimbursement rules; elsewhere it becomes a headline.

C. Reimburse everyone in full, no questions asked.  
   _What happens:_ Victims are grateful. Within a month, fraudsters start staging fake scam claims.

**Lesson.** Reimbursement needs a consistent, documented policy that protects vulnerable customers without rewarding fake claims.

**The law behind it.** Since October 2024, UK payment firms must reimburse most authorised push payment scam victims up to £85,000, splitting the cost 50:50 between sending and receiving firms. There's an exception for gross negligence by the customer.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Closing the gap

The scammers spoofed your number and used customer details from an old data breach.

A. Change your support phone number.  
   _What happens:_ Scammers spoof the new one within a week.

B. **Work with phone carriers to block spoofing of your numbers, and show in the app when a call really is from you.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Impersonation calls drop sharply.

C. Tell customers to be more careful in your newsletter.  
   _What happens:_ Few people read it.

**Lesson.** Impersonation scams need channel fixes: authenticated calls, carrier partnerships and in-app confirmation.

**The law behind it.** Caller authentication programs such as STIR/SHAKEN in the US, and similar schemes elsewhere, help carriers block spoofed calls that impersonate trusted brands.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

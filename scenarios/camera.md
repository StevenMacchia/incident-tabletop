# The hidden camera

**Sev 2** · Short-term rental platform · For: Gig, delivery & rentals

> A guest finds a hidden camera in a rental bedroom and posts the video. It has millions of views, and the host has 40 other listings.

## Why this scenario

In May 2025 a US House Oversight subcommittee held a hearing on covert surveillance in private spaces. It said these include hotel rooms and short-term rentals. Source: [US House Oversight Committee](https://oversight.house.gov/release/hearing-wrap-up-action-needed-to-prevent-covert-surveillance-in-private-spaces), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Sun 14:00: A viral video

A guest posts a video of a camera hidden in a smoke detector in the bedroom of a listing. It has 3 million views. The host has 40 other listings.

A. Remove just this one listing.  
   _What happens:_ Guests at the host's other properties find similar devices.

B. Reply publicly that you'll investigate within five business days.  
   _What happens:_ The reply goes viral for the wrong reasons.

C. **Suspend all the host's listings, contact the guest, move them to safe accommodation and preserve evidence.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The guest thanks you publicly, and police take the device as evidence.

**Lesson.** A serious privacy violation by a host says something about all their listings. Act on the whole account, and support the guest first.

## Decision 2 · Mon 09:00: Past guests

1,200 guests have stayed at the host's properties in the past two years.

A. **Tell past guests what happened and how to contact the police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Several come forward, strengthening the police case.

B. Say nothing to past guests: there's no proof they were recorded.  
   _What happens:_ The press finds past guests first.

C. Send past guests discount codes.  
   _What happens:_ It reads as buying their silence.

**Lesson.** Affected people deserve to know. Proactive notification is also often expected by law for privacy harms.

**The law behind it.** Hidden recording in private spaces is a criminal offence in many places, including under US and UK voyeurism laws. Privacy law also generally expects affected people to be told when their data or images may have been captured.

## Decision 3 · Tue 11:00: The camera policy

Your policy allows outdoor cameras if disclosed, but many hosts don't disclose them and nobody checks.

A. Ban all cameras, including doorbells and outdoor security.  
   _What happens:_ Hosts push back hard, and some safety value is lost.

B. **Ban cameras in any indoor space, require disclosure of outdoor devices, and add a guest checklist and fast reporting.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The rules are clear; reports of undisclosed cameras rise and are handled quickly.

C. Keep the policy and remind hosts.  
   _What happens:_ Another incident follows.

**Lesson.** Bright-line rules are easier to enforce and easier for guests to understand.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Rebuilding trust

Bookings in the region are down 6%.

A. Run a discount campaign.  
   _What happens:_ Bookings rise briefly, but trust doesn't.

B. Pay influencers to post about positive stays.  
   _What happens:_ It's exposed as paid content and backfires.

C. **Publish a guest safety report with incident numbers and the new rules.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Bookings recover as coverage turns positive.

**Lesson.** Being open about incidents and fixes rebuilds trust better than marketing.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

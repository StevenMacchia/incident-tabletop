# The swatting threat

**Sev 1** · Online multiplayer game · For: Gaming, Social media & video

> A popular streamer is hit by a coordinated harassment campaign that escalates to posting their home address.

## Why this scenario

A California teenager sentenced in February 2025 made over 375 swatting and threat calls between August 2022 and January 2024 and advertised swatting-for-a-fee on social media, the US Justice Department said. Source: [US Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/california-teenager-sentenced-48-months-prison-nationwide-swatting-spree), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Sat 21:00: A harassment campaign

A streamer with 500,000 followers is being flooded with slurs in voice and text chat across dozens of matches. Hundreds of brand-new accounts are involved.

A. **Limit new accounts from joining the streamer's matches, tighten chat filters in those lobbies, and ban the organizing accounts.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The flood slows within the hour.

B. Tell the streamer to use the block button.  
   _What happens:_ They can't block hundreds of accounts mid-match. The campaign continues.

C. Turn off chat across the whole game for the night.  
   _What happens:_ The harassment stops, and so does everyone else's game.

**Lesson.** Coordinated harassment needs coordinated defenses: limits on new accounts, targeted filtering and action against organizers.

## Decision 2 · Sat 23:30: The address is posted

Someone posts the streamer's home address in match chat and on a forum, saying they'll “send a SWAT team”.

A. Remove the post and ban the account.  
   _What happens:_ The post is gone, but police aren't warned.

B. Wait until Monday for your legal team.  
   _What happens:_ A SWAT team arrives at the streamer's home at 3 a.m.

C. **Remove the content, preserve evidence, warn local police about a possible swatting call, and tell the streamer.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Police flag the address, and a hoax call later that night is handled calmly.

**Lesson.** Threats to physical safety need a 24/7 emergency path to law enforcement.

**The law behind it.** US law (18 U.S.C. § 2702(b)(8)) lets providers disclose data to law enforcement when they believe in good faith that an emergency involves danger of death or serious injury. Many other countries have similar emergency provisions.

## Decision 3 · Sun 12:00: Where did it come from?

The attackers organized in a server on another chat platform and used your game's party system to coordinate.

A. Ban only the accounts that sent slurs.  
   _What happens:_ The organizers regroup under new accounts.

B. **Share the organizing accounts with the other platform's trust & safety team and ban the linked accounts in your game.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The other platform removes the server, and the campaign collapses.

C. Name the organizers publicly.  
   _What happens:_ The feud escalates, and the organizers gain followers.

**Lesson.** Harassment is usually organized somewhere else. Cross-platform cooperation and action against organizers break campaigns.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Protecting creators

Other streamers ask what you'll do to protect them.

A. Say harassment comes with being online.  
   _What happens:_ Creators leave for a competitor.

B. Pay creators to keep streaming.  
   _What happens:_ Some stay, but they're still unprotected.

C. **Start a creator safety program: priority escalation, lobby protections and private support.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Creators feel supported, and several who planned to leave stay.

**Lesson.** Highly visible users attract concentrated abuse. A dedicated escalation path costs little and builds a lot of trust.

# The listing that didn't exist

**Sev 2** · Short-term rental platform · For: Gig, delivery & rentals

> Holiday travelers arrive at rentals that don't exist, after being talked into paying outside your platform.

## Why this scenario

UK victims made 6,066 reports of holiday fraud to Action Fraud in 2024 and lost £11,183,957. Police advise that a request to pay by bank transfer can be a sign of fraud. Source: [Action Fraud (City of London Police)](https://www.atol.org/news-and-blogs/2025/02/24/dont-let-fraudsters-trip-you-up-this-summer-new-alert-issued-as-people-look-to-snap-up-online-holiday-deals/), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Fri 18:00: Guests arrive at empty lots

Twelve families arrive for holiday stays to find the addresses are empty lots or someone else's home. Each booking started on your platform, but the “host” offered a discount for paying by bank transfer.

A. Tell the families they broke the rules by paying off-platform.  
   _What happens:_ Families are stranded, and the story trends.

B. **Find emergency accommodation for the families tonight, and remove the fake listings and linked accounts.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Every family has somewhere to stay by midnight.

C. Remove the listings and deal with the families on Monday.  
   _What happens:_ Families sleep in their cars.

**Lesson.** People in immediate need come first, even when they broke your rules.

## Decision 2 · Sat 10:00: How many more?

The fake listings used stolen photos of real properties, and the same bank details appear in messages across 80 listings.

A. Remove only the listings that get reported.  
   _What happens:_ Holiday travelers keep arriving at empty addresses.

B. Suspend all new listings until after the holidays.  
   _What happens:_ Real hosts lose bookings in peak season.

C. **Search for listings with reused photos and the same payment details in messages, remove them, and warn guests with upcoming bookings.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You remove 80 listings and warn 300 guests before they pay.

**Lesson.** Scam listings share fingerprints such as reused photos and payment details. Hunt for them proactively.

## Decision 3 · Sun 11:00: The money

The families paid a total of $60,000 by bank transfer.

A. **Reimburse the families from a goodwill fund, and share the scammers' bank details with banks and police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Banks freeze two of the scammers' accounts.

B. Tell the families to contact their banks.  
   _What happens:_ Most recover nothing.

C. Reimburse only families who complain publicly.  
   _What happens:_ Word gets out, and trust falls.

**Lesson.** Supporting victims of scams that start on your platform is part of your trust promise.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Stopping fake listings

Leadership asks how to prevent fake listings.

A. Add “never pay outside the app” to booking confirmations.  
   _What happens:_ Some guests notice it; most don't.

B. Require every host to meet staff in person.  
   _What happens:_ It's too costly to scale.

C. **Verify that new hosts own or manage the property, detect reused photos, and warn guests when messages mention paying elsewhere.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Fake listings and off-platform payment scams drop sharply.

**Lesson.** Verify property control for new hosts, and step into conversations at the moment of risk.

**The law behind it.** The EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (applying from May 2026) requires hosts to show a registration number and platforms to check it, which is a useful signal against fake listings.

# The coerced customer

**Sev 2** · Retail bank app · For: Fintech & payments

> A live-in carer appears to be draining an 84-year-old customer's account, and she insists everything is fine.

## Decision 1 · Mon 11:00: A worrying pattern

An 84-year-old customer's account shows $38,000 in new cash withdrawals and transfers to a new payee over six weeks. A new phone number and email were added last month. The payee is her live-in carer.

A. Do nothing: she authorized the payments.  
   _What happens:_ The account is nearly empty two weeks later.

B. **Pause large transfers to the new payee, and have a trained specialist call her alone on her original phone number.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ On the call, she mentions being frightened of the carer.

C. Close the account to stop the losses.  
   _What happens:_ She loses access to her pension and bills, and the carer opens a new account elsewhere.

**Lesson.** Financial abuse of older people often hides behind “authorized” payments. Talk to the customer alone, through a channel you trust.

## Decision 2 · Mon 16:00: She won't make a complaint

She's frightened, but won't report the carer, who is her only help at home.

A. **With her consent, involve adult protective services, add extra checks on large payments, and give her a named contact at the bank.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Social services arrange other care, and the transfers stop.

B. Respect her decision and do nothing more.  
   _What happens:_ The abuse continues.

C. Report the carer to police without telling her.  
   _What happens:_ She feels betrayed and stops talking to you.

**Lesson.** Respect the customer's choices while reducing harm: involve safeguarding services and add protections with her agreement.

**The law behind it.** In the US, the Senior Safe Act (2018) protects trained financial staff who report suspected elder financial exploitation in good faith, and many states require or allow reports to adult protective services.

## Decision 3 · Tue 10:00: The carer calls

The carer calls, angry that payments are blocked, and says he has power of attorney.

A. Take his word for it and unblock the payments.  
   _What happens:_ He has no such document.

B. Tell him you suspect abuse.  
   _What happens:_ He becomes aggressive toward her at home.

C. **Ask for the legal document and have your legal team verify it before changing anything.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The document doesn't exist.

**Lesson.** Verify any claimed legal authority, and never share your suspicions with a suspected abuser.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: A vulnerable-customer policy

Your team realizes there's no process for spotting or handling financial abuse.

A. Rely on staff judgment.  
   _What happens:_ The next case goes unnoticed.

B. **Create a vulnerable-customer policy with abuse indicators, trained specialists, trusted-contact options and links to local safeguarding services.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The team spots three more cases the following quarter.

C. Block all transfers for customers over 80.  
   _What happens:_ It's discriminatory, and customers complain.

**Lesson.** Financial abuse needs a defined process: warning signs, trained staff and trusted contacts the customer chooses.

**The law behind it.** The UK Financial Conduct Authority's Consumer Duty expects firms to identify customers in vulnerable circumstances and adapt how they treat them.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

# The recreated attack

**Sev 1** · Game with user-built worlds · For: Gaming

> A user-built world recreating a real mass shooting is trending among teenage players.

## Why this scenario

In a November 2025 action on gaming and gaming-related platforms, Europol and eight countries referred about 5,408 jihadist and 1,070 violent right-wing extremist links; Europol says perpetrators re-enact terrorist attacks and school shootings in 3D gameplay. Source: [Europol](https://www.europol.europa.eu/media-press/newsroom/news/europol-and-partner-countries-combat-online-radicalisation-gaming-platforms), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Fri 16:00: A trending world

A user-built world recreating last month's mass shooting, with the attacker's manifesto on the walls, has 80,000 visits, mostly from teenagers. A news outlet has just noticed.

A. **Take the world down, preserve it as evidence, and ban the creator's account.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ It's gone within minutes, and copies are blocked by matching the world's assets.

B. Add an age warning to the world.  
   _What happens:_ Visits double, and the story is about your warning label.

C. Review it on Monday with the policy team.  
   _What happens:_ Copies spread across the weekend.

**Lesson.** Content glorifying real attacks should come down immediately, with evidence preserved and copies blocked.

## Decision 2 · Fri 20:00: Copies and a network

Twelve copies have appeared. The creator belongs to a group that has built other extremist worlds and recruits players into a private chat server.

A. Remove copies as they're reported.  
   _What happens:_ New copies appear faster than the reports.

B. **Match copies by shared assets and scripts, remove the group's other worlds, and refer the recruitment to law enforcement and industry partners.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The group's presence is removed in one sweep, and industry partners are alerted.

C. Shut down world-building for everyone this weekend.  
   _What happens:_ Millions of legitimate creators are blocked.

**Lesson.** Extremist content comes from networks. Match copies by their parts and act against the whole network.

**The law behind it.** GIFCT's hash-sharing database and Content Incident Protocol help platforms block copies of attack-related content quickly. In the EU, the Terrorist Content Online Regulation requires removal within one hour of an authority's order.

## Decision 3 · Sat 11:00: Players who visited

80,000 players visited, many of them young, and some joined the recruitment server.

A. Do nothing more: the content is gone.  
   _What happens:_ Some young players are drawn further into the group elsewhere.

B. **Show visitors a message with counter-extremism resources, and redirect searches about the attack to credible information.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Several parents contact counter-extremism services for help.

C. Ban all 80,000 visitors.  
   _What happens:_ Curious teenagers are punished, and parents are furious.

**Lesson.** People exposed to extremist content may need redirection and support, not punishment.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Prevention

Leadership asks how to catch the next one before it trends.

A. Require approval for every new world.  
   _What happens:_ Reviews take weeks, and creators leave.

B. Rely on player reports.  
   _What happens:_ The next world reaches 50,000 visits first.

C. **Check new worlds' names, text and assets against extremist indicators, and review fast-rising worlds before they reach recommendations.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Two similar worlds are caught before they trend.

**Lesson.** Proactive detection plus a check before content is amplified catches harm before it spreads.

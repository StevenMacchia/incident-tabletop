# The counterfeit flood

**Sev 2** · Online resale marketplace · For: Marketplace & e-commerce

> A luxury brand's lawyers demand that 1,400 listings come down in 48 hours, days before your biggest sales event.

## Decision 1 · Mon 09:00: A brand's legal team sends a demand

A luxury brand has listed 1,400 listings it says are counterfeit and threatens to sue if they aren't removed in 48 hours. Your Black Friday sale starts Thursday. Spot checks suggest about 70% are clear fakes; the rest are genuine resale or unclear.

A. Remove all 1,400 immediately to make the problem go away.  
   _What happens:_ The brand is satisfied, but hundreds of legitimate resellers lose sales, flood support and post about it.

B. **Remove clear fakes now, hold unclear listings for joint review with the brand, and tell sellers why.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most fakes are down within a day. The brand's counsel accepts the plan and genuine resellers keep selling.

C. Ask the brand to file individual notices through your standard IP form.  
   _What happens:_ It's defensible, but the brand goes public and the fakes stay up through the sale.

**Lesson.** Pressure from a rights holder doesn't remove the need for accuracy. Act fast on clear cases, review the unclear ones, and explain decisions to sellers.

**The law behind it.** Notice-and-action rules (EU DSA Article 16) require timely, diligent and objective decisions on notices, not blanket removal. In the US, trademark takedowns aren't covered by the DMCA's safe harbor, so an accurate, documented process matters.

## Decision 2 · Tue 14:00: The same sellers keep coming back

Removed sellers are relisting within hours under new accounts, with the same photos and shipping addresses. Your seller checks only verify an email address.

A. Ban each new account as reports come in.  
   _What happens:_ Reviewers spend the day chasing relists while new ones appear.

B. Pause all new listings in the affected categories for the week.  
   _What happens:_ The fakes stop, and so do legitimate new sellers in the biggest sales week of the year.

C. **Link accounts by photo hashes, shipping addresses and payout details, and remove the whole network.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ One sweep removes 60 linked accounts, and relisting slows sharply.

**Lesson.** Counterfeiters operate as networks. Remove the network using shared signals instead of chasing single listings.

## Decision 3 · Wed 11:00: Buyers want their money back

About 900 buyers have already received fakes. Some want refunds, and a consumer group says you're profiting from counterfeits.

A. **Refund affected buyers proactively and recover the money from held seller payouts.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Held payouts cover most refunds, and the consumer group acknowledges the response.

B. Tell buyers to take it up with the sellers directly.  
   _What happens:_ The sellers have vanished. Buyers go to their banks and chargebacks pile up.

C. Refund only buyers who complain publicly.  
   _What happens:_ It quiets the loudest voices, until a screenshot of the policy leaks.

**Lesson.** Buyer protection is part of your promise. Holding payouts for new sellers is what makes it affordable to make buyers whole.

## Decision 4 · Following Mon: After the sale

The sale is over. The brand wants a standing agreement, and your CEO wants to know why this happened.

A. Give the brand direct takedown access to your listings.  
   _What happens:_ Takedowns are fast, but the brand starts removing legitimate resale and you face complaints and legal risk.

B. **Launch a brand protection program: a verified rights-holder portal, a repeat-infringer policy and stronger checks in high-risk categories.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Takedowns get faster and more accurate, and fakes drop before the holidays.

C. Treat it as a one-off and move on.  
   _What happens:_ The same networks return for the next sale.

**Lesson.** Rights holders are partners, not moderators. A structured program with accuracy checks protects both brands and legitimate sellers.

**The law behind it.** Under the DSA (Article 22), notices from officially designated trusted flaggers get priority, but the platform still makes the decision.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

# The romance scam ring

**Sev 2** · Dating app · For: Dating

> An organized ring is running romance and investment scams through your app with AI-generated profiles.

## Why this scenario

Cryptocurrency investment fraud cost Americans $7.2 billion in 2025, the largest source of losses the FBI recorded. Scammers often make first contact through dating apps or social media, then move victims to messaging apps. Source: [FBI IC3 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf), 2026.

## Decision 1 · Wed 10:00: Complaints spike

Reports of users being pushed into crypto “investments” after weeks of chatting have tripled. Many suspect profiles use flawless AI-generated photos and near-identical opening lines.

A. Remove reported profiles one at a time.  
   _What happens:_ New profiles appear faster than you can remove them.

B. Suspend every profile without a verification badge.  
   _What happens:_ Scams drop, and so do half your real users' matches.

C. **Cluster the profiles by shared signals (generated-photo traits, scripts, devices, IP ranges) and remove the whole ring.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ 2,400 accounts come down in one sweep.

**Lesson.** Organized scam rings leave shared fingerprints. Remove them at the network level.

**The law behind it.** Romance fraud falls under fraud, a priority illegal harm in the UK Online Safety Act. Ofcom's codes expect proactive measures such as detecting fake and scam accounts.

## Decision 2 · Wed 16:00: Victims are still talking to them

Thousands of users were in active conversations with the removed accounts, and some have already sent money.

A. **Tell users who chatted with the removed accounts what happened, explain the scam, and link to support and reporting resources.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Many realize they were being scammed and stop sending money.

B. Say nothing: the accounts are gone.  
   _What happens:_ Victims keep talking to the scammers on the messaging app they'd already moved to.

C. Refund everyone's subscription.  
   _What happens:_ It's appreciated, but it doesn't touch their losses.

**Lesson.** Removing scammers isn't enough. Warn the people they targeted, because the scam usually continues off-platform.

## Decision 3 · Thu 11:00: The verification debate

Product wants to make photo verification mandatory for new users, which is estimated to cut sign-ups by 8%.

A. Make verification mandatory for everyone today.  
   _What happens:_ Scams fall, and sign-ups drop more than expected.

B. Keep verification optional and change nothing.  
   _What happens:_ The ring returns next month with new profiles.

C. **Require verification for accounts showing risk signals, and promote a verified badge for everyone else.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most scam accounts are stopped with little effect on sign-ups.

**Lesson.** Risk-based friction targets bad actors while keeping things smooth for everyone else.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Following the money

Victims' reported losses total $3 million, paid into crypto wallets.

A. Treat it as outside your responsibility, since payments happened elsewhere.  
   _What happens:_ Victims and regulators disagree.

B. **Share wallet addresses and indicators with law enforcement, crypto exchanges and industry groups.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Exchanges freeze some funds, and two victims recover money.

C. Hire a fund-recovery firm for victims.  
   _What happens:_ Some “recovery firms” are scams themselves, and victims are targeted again.

**Lesson.** Scam losses happen off-platform but start on yours. Sharing intelligence is how platforms help victims recover.

**The law behind it.** Sharing threat indicators with industry groups and law enforcement is how cross-platform scam networks get disrupted.

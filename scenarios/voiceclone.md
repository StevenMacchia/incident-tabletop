# The cloned voice

**Sev 1** · AI voice tool · For: Generative AI

> Your voice tool was used to clone a teenager's voice for a fake kidnapping call to her parents.

## Why this scenario

Victims told the FBI they lost over $5 million in 2025 to 'distress' scams, where voice cloning mimics a loved one in trouble. Source: [FBI IC3 Annual Report](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf), 2026.

## Decision 1 · Sat 14:00: A fake kidnapping call

Police contact you: a family received a call in their daughter's cloned voice, crying for help, followed by a ransom demand. The clone was made with your tool from a video on her social media.

A. **Suspend the account that made the clone, preserve all logs and audio, and give police what they need through your legal process.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Police trace the payment account linked to the user.

B. Point out that your terms forbid impersonation.  
   _What happens:_ Police get nothing useful, and the family goes to the press.

C. Delete the cloned voice.  
   _What happens:_ Evidence is lost.

**Lesson.** When your tool is used in a crime, preserve evidence and support the investigation quickly.

## Decision 2 · Sun 10:00: How easy was it?

Your tool can clone any voice from 30 seconds of audio, with no check that the speaker agreed.

A. Add a checkbox saying “I have permission”.  
   _What happens:_ Everyone ticks it.

B. Shut down voice cloning permanently.  
   _What happens:_ Legitimate uses, such as preserving the voices of people losing their speech, disappear.

C. **Require consent from the speaker: a live recorded statement from the person being cloned, matched against the sample.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Cloning other people's voices becomes much harder.

**Lesson.** Verifying the speaker's consent is the key safeguard for voice cloning. A checkbox isn't consent.

## Decision 3 · Mon 11:00: Other misuse

A review finds cloned voices of politicians and CEOs being used in scam calls and fake endorsements.

A. Remove the specific clones you found.  
   _What happens:_ New ones are made the same day.

B. **Block cloning of known public figures, add inaudible watermarks to all generated audio, and give banks and platforms a detection tool.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Banks start flagging cloned audio in fraud calls.

C. Publish a warning about voice scams.  
   _What happens:_ It helps a little.

**Lesson.** Watermarking and detection help the whole ecosystem, not only your platform.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Regulation

Lawmakers are proposing rules on voice cloning and AI impersonation.

A. Lobby against any regulation.  
   _What happens:_ Your company becomes the example lawmakers point to.

B. **Share your consent and watermarking approach with lawmakers, and meet disclosure rules early.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Your approach is cited in the draft rules.

C. Wait and see.  
   _What happens:_ Rules arrive that are harder to meet at short notice.

**Lesson.** Helping shape sensible rules with working safeguards beats reacting to rules written after an incident.

**The law behind it.** In 2024 the US FCC ruled that AI-generated voices in robocalls fall under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act. The EU AI Act requires AI-generated or manipulated audio to be disclosed.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

# The sanctions near-miss

**Sev 2** · Payments and crypto app · For: Fintech & payments

> Customers have been sending money to crypto wallets that were just added to a sanctions list, and three transfers are pending.

## Decision 1 · Tue 08:00: A new sanctions listing

Twelve crypto wallets were added to the US sanctions list overnight. Customers sent $2.4 million to two of them in the past month, and three transfers to them are pending right now.

A. **Block the pending transfers, freeze the accounts involved while you review, and escalate to your compliance officer.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ No new money reaches the sanctioned wallets, and compliance begins its review within the hour.

B. Let the pending transfers go through: they started before the listing.  
   _What happens:_ They complete after the listing took effect, which is itself a violation.

C. Close every account that has ever sent crypto.  
   _What happens:_ Thousands of legitimate customers lose access, and complaints flood in.

**Lesson.** Sanctions apply from the moment of listing. Screen against updates in near real time and stop pending payments.

**The law behind it.** US sanctions administered by OFAC are strict liability: violations can be penalized even without intent. Blocked or rejected transactions must generally be reported to OFAC within 10 business days.

## Decision 2 · Tue 13:00: Do you report it?

Compliance confirms the earlier transfers happened before the listing, but the pending ones were blocked after it.

A. **File the required reports on the blocked transfers within the deadline, with a clear timeline.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The regulator acknowledges the report and takes no further action.

B. Say nothing, because no money reached the wallets.  
   _What happens:_ A later audit finds the unreported blocks, and the penalty is worse for the silence.

C. Report everything to every regulator worldwide.  
   _What happens:_ It creates confusion and extra work with no benefit.

**Lesson.** Blocked sanctioned transactions often have to be reported too. Know the reporting duties everywhere you operate.

## Decision 3 · Wed 10:00: The customers

Two of the frozen customers look like legitimate small businesses, and one was a victim of a scam run through the sanctioned wallet.

A. Keep everyone frozen indefinitely.  
   _What happens:_ Legitimate businesses can't pay staff, and complaints reach the regulator.

B. **Review each case quickly, unfreeze clearly legitimate customers, and help the scam victim report to police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Legitimate customers are back within days, and the scam victim thanks you.

C. Unfreeze everyone to reduce complaints.  
   _What happens:_ One of them turns out to be linked to the sanctioned network.

**Lesson.** Sanctions freezes need fast case-by-case review, so legitimate customers aren't punished for a match.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: The screening gap

Your screening checked customers at sign-up, but never the addresses they send money to.

A. Keep screening customers only at sign-up.  
   _What happens:_ The same gap is exploited again.

B. **Screen every outgoing payment against updated sanctions lists, and use blockchain analytics for crypto addresses.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The next listing is caught automatically within minutes.

C. Stop supporting crypto transfers.  
   _What happens:_ Customers leave for competitors.

**Lesson.** Sanctions screening has to cover transactions and counterparties, not just customers at sign-up.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

# The courier at the door

**Sev 1** · Food delivery platform · For: Gig, delivery & rentals

> A customer reports being assaulted by a courier. The courier's account turns out to have been rented to someone else.

## Why this scenario

The UK Home Office said in July 2025 that delivery platforms' crackdown on illegal account sharing had already led to thousands being offboarded. It also announced a deal for the platforms to detect shared accounts and suspend them faster. Source: [UK Home Office](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-operational-partnership-with-delivery-giants-to-combat-illegal-working), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Fri 22:00: A report from a customer

A customer reports that the courier who delivered her order grabbed her at the door. The account belongs to a verified courier, but his photo doesn't match her description.

A. Ask the customer to confirm the details before acting.  
   _What happens:_ The account completes 20 more deliveries overnight.

B. **Deactivate the account now, contact the customer to offer support, preserve trip data and cooperate with police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Police identify the person from trip data within a day.

C. Send the courier a warning.  
   _What happens:_ The warning reaches the account owner, who wasn't the person at the door.

**Lesson.** For physical-safety reports, act first to prevent further harm, then investigate. Preserve trip data for police.

## Decision 2 · Sat 10:00: Account renting

The verified courier admits renting his account to someone who couldn't pass a background check. About 3% of active couriers log in from unusual devices.

A. Deactivate every courier with an unusual login.  
   _What happens:_ Many honest couriers who changed phones lose income.

B. Email all couriers about the rules.  
   _What happens:_ Account renting carries on.

C. **Require a selfie check at the start of shifts for accounts with risk signals.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You find 400 shared accounts in a week.

**Lesson.** Account sharing defeats background checks. Re-verify identity at the moment of work, targeted by risk.

## Decision 3 · Sat 16:00: Couriers push back

Courier groups say the selfie checks are invasive and deactivations are unfair, with no way to appeal.

A. **Explain why the checks exist, add a fast human appeal for deactivations, and publish how decisions are made.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Most couriers accept the process, and wrongful deactivations are reversed within 48 hours.

B. Ignore them: safety comes first.  
   _What happens:_ Couriers protest, and a regulator asks about due process.

C. Drop the selfie checks.  
   _What happens:_ Account renting is back within weeks.

**Lesson.** Enforcement that affects people's income needs a clear explanation and a real appeal.

**The law behind it.** The EU Platform Work Directive, which countries must apply by December 2026, requires human review of significant automated decisions such as deactivations, with a right to an explanation.

## Decision 4 · Week 3: Customer safety

Customers ask how they can know the courier at their door is the right person.

A. Recommend customers choose “leave at door”.  
   _What happens:_ It helps some people, but doesn't solve identity.

B. **Show the courier's verified photo and name in the app, and add a safety button that connects to support and emergency services.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Customers use it and report mismatches faster.

C. Do nothing more: the offender was caught.  
   _What happens:_ A similar incident happens in another city.

**Lesson.** Give both sides of an in-person service the tools to verify each other and get help.

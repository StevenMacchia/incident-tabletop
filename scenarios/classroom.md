# The image in the class chat

**Sev 1** · School learning app · For: Kids & education

> A student shares an explicit image of a classmate in your app's class chat. Teachers and parents want answers.

## Decision 1 · Tue 11:00: An image in the class chat

A 14-year-old posted a nude image of a classmate in a class group chat during school hours. Thirty students saw it before a teacher reported it.

A. Delete the image and the chat, and consider the matter closed.  
   _What happens:_ Evidence is lost and the school isn't told.

B. **Remove the image, report it to NCMEC as required, preserve evidence and alert the school's safeguarding lead.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The school's safeguarding process starts within the hour, and the image is blocked by hash.

C. Suspend the whole class chat until further notice.  
   _What happens:_ The image lives on in screenshots, and lessons are disrupted.

**Lesson.** Sexual images of minors must be reported even when the sender is a minor. Bring the school in through its safeguarding process.

**The law behind it.** Reporting duties for apparent child sexual abuse material apply even when the sender is a minor (in the US, 18 U.S.C. § 2258A).

## Decision 2 · Tue 15:00: Screenshots are spreading

Students took screenshots and are sharing the image in other chats on your app.

A. Ask teachers to tell students to delete it.  
   _What happens:_ Some do. Many don't.

B. Remove image uploads for every user.  
   _What happens:_ Teachers lose a core feature for lessons.

C. **Hash-match the image across the app to block re-uploads, and help the victim submit it to NCMEC's Take It Down.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Re-shares are blocked automatically, including on other participating platforms.

**Lesson.** Hash matching stops re-sharing at scale, and Take It Down extends protection beyond your own app.

**The law behind it.** NCMEC's Take It Down lets minors create a digital fingerprint of an image so participating platforms can find and remove it, without the image itself being shared.

## Decision 3 · Wed 10:00: Parents demand changes

Parents ask why students can send images to each other at all.

A. **Turn off image sharing between students by default, keep teacher-approved sharing, and add nudity detection on images.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Parents and schools welcome the change.

B. Explain that the student broke the rules.  
   _What happens:_ Parents feel dismissed.

C. Remove student chat entirely.  
   _What happens:_ Group work stops working, and schools consider switching.

**Lesson.** Protective defaults for children, with exceptions controlled by adults, balance safety and learning.

## Decision 4 · Week 2: Your school customers

School districts ask for your safeguarding policy before renewing contracts.

A. Send a one-page assurance letter.  
   _What happens:_ Two districts pause renewals to ask more questions.

B. **Publish a safeguarding policy with incident procedures, response times and commitments to notify schools.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Renewals go ahead, and the policy becomes a sales advantage.

C. Offer a discount instead.  
   _What happens:_ Districts still ask for the policy.

**Lesson.** For children's products, safeguarding documentation is part of what customers are buying.

**The law behind it.** Schools must meet their own duties when using edtech: FERPA and COPPA in the US, and the Keeping Children Safe in Education guidance in England.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

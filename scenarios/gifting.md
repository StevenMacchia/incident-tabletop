# The gifts that weren't gifts

**Sev 1** · Kids' multiplayer game · For: Gaming, Kids & education

> A parent reports an adult who befriended their 11-year-old in your game with in-game gifts, then moved the chat to another app.

## Why this scenario

In a 2024 Thorn survey of 1,200 US 13-20-year-olds, 1 in 4 said that while under 18 they had been asked online for a sexual image or interaction in exchange for something of value. Source: [Thorn, Commodified Sexual Interactions Involving Minors](https://info.thorn.org/hubfs/Research/Thorn_CommodifiedSexualInteractionsInvolvingMinors_Apr2025.pdf), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Tue 19:00: A parent's report

A parent emails that an adult player gifted their 11-year-old in-game currency for weeks, then asked them to chat on another app and send photos. They have screenshots. The adult's account is still active.

A. Reply that you'll look into it within your standard three-day review time.  
   _What happens:_ The account keeps messaging children for three more days.

B. **Escalate now: suspend the account, preserve chats and gift records, and report to NCMEC or the police.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The account is stopped within the hour, and evidence is preserved for investigators.

C. Ban the account and delete its data.  
   _What happens:_ The account is stopped, but the evidence police need is gone.

**Lesson.** Child-safety reports need their own fast lane. Suspend, preserve evidence and report. Never delete evidence.

**The law behind it.** US law (18 U.S.C. § 2258A) requires reporting apparent child sexual exploitation to NCMEC's CyberTipline. The 2024 REPORT Act added online enticement to the reporting duty and extended evidence preservation to one year.

## Decision 2 · Wed 10:00: There are more

The account's gift history shows currency sent to 40 other child accounts, and it's linked by device to six other adult accounts.

A. Ban the linked accounts and close the case.  
   _What happens:_ The other children who were contacted never get support.

B. Wait for other parents to report.  
   _What happens:_ Few children report. The harm stays hidden.

C. **Review all 40 conversations and the six linked accounts, and contact the affected families through their parent accounts.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ You find two more children who were asked for images, and their families can act.

**Lesson.** One report is often the tip of a pattern. Follow the network and reach the other children affected.

## Decision 3 · Wed 16:00: Should gifting continue?

Your data shows adults gifting currency to children they aren't connected to is rare, but strongly linked to confirmed grooming cases.

A. **Block gifts from adults to children they aren't connected to, and flag attempts for review.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The lure is closed with little effect on normal play.

B. Turn off gifting for everyone.  
   _What happens:_ The lure is gone, but so is a feature families love, and revenue dips.

C. Keep gifting as it is: it's core to the game.  
   _What happens:_ Two months later, the pattern appears again.

**Lesson.** Remove the specific mechanics predators exploit, targeting risky combinations rather than whole features.

## Decision 4 · Week 3: Talking to parents

Parents are worried, and a news outlet asks whether your game is safe for children.

A. Say you have “zero tolerance” and decline to give details.  
   _What happens:_ The reporter notes you didn't describe any changes.

B. Say the incident was isolated and rare.  
   _What happens:_ Two more parents come forward the next day.

C. **Launch parental controls (chat off by default for under-13s, gift limits, activity summaries) and explain them clearly.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Parents adopt the controls, and the coverage mentions them.

**Lesson.** Give parents real controls and honest explanations. Reassurance without action backfires.

**The law behind it.** In 2022 the FTC fined Epic Games $275 million under COPPA, citing voice and text chat switched on by default for children. The UK Age Appropriate Design Code also expects high-privacy defaults for children.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.

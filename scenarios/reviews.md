# The review factory

**Sev 2** · Online marketplace · For: Marketplace & e-commerce

> A competitor sends proof that a rival seller is paying for thousands of five-star reviews through a private group.

## Why this scenario

In December 2025 the FTC warned 10 companies about possible violations of its Consumer Review Rule on deceptive reviews. Each violation can bring a civil penalty of up to $53,088. Source: [US Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-warns-10-companies-about-possible-violations-agencys-new-consumer-review-rule), 2025.

## Decision 1 · Mon 10:00: A competitor's evidence

A seller sends screenshots of a private group where people are paid $10, refunded after posting, for each five-star review of a rival seller's products. About 3,000 reviews look connected.

A. Ignore it: competitors complain about each other all the time.  
   _What happens:_ The scheme grows, and a consumer group publishes the same screenshots.

B. **Investigate the reviews using purchase patterns, refunds and reviewer networks, and freeze the rival's ratings while you do.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Within two days you confirm 2,600 paid reviews.

C. Suspend the rival seller immediately on the competitor's word.  
   _What happens:_ Some screenshots turn out to be fabricated, and the seller sues.

**Lesson.** Tips from competitors are useful but aren't proof. Investigate with your own data before acting.

## Decision 2 · Wed 11:00: Confirmed

The evidence is clear: 2,600 reviews were paid for, and the seller made $400,000 from the boosted sales.

A. **Remove the fake reviews, suspend the seller, and hold their payouts while you decide on refunds.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The listings drop out of the rankings, and money is held back for affected buyers.

B. Remove the fake reviews but keep the seller.  
   _What happens:_ The seller buys a new batch of reviews next month.

C. Leave the reviews up and warn the seller.  
   _What happens:_ Buyers keep relying on fake ratings.

**Lesson.** Paid reviews defraud buyers. Remove them and act against the account that paid for them.

## Decision 3 · Thu 09:00: The reviewers

Most of the 900 reviewers are real people who were paid. Some post paid reviews for many sellers.

A. Ban all 900 reviewers.  
   _What happens:_ Many were one-off participants, and you lose real customers.

B. **Remove their reviews, ban the repeat reviewers who work for many sellers, and warn one-time participants.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ The professional reviewers are gone, and most one-time participants stop.

C. Take no action against reviewers.  
   _What happens:_ The same reviewers are hired by other sellers next week.

**Lesson.** Tell professional review farms apart from one-off participants, and act in proportion.

## Decision 4 · Month 2: Preventing the next scheme

Fake reviews are now banned by consumer-protection law in the US, UK and EU, with fines attached.

A. **Let only verified buyers review, detect review bursts and refund patterns, and publish your review policy.** ✅ strongest call  
   _What happens:_ Paid-review schemes become much harder, and regulators see a documented program.

B. Hide review counts.  
   _What happens:_ Buyers trust the ratings even less.

C. Rely on buyers to report fake reviews.  
   _What happens:_ Most fake reviews are never reported.

**Lesson.** Review integrity is now a legal requirement in many markets, not only a quality issue.

**The law behind it.** The US FTC's 2024 rule on consumer reviews bans fake reviews and review suppression, with civil penalties. The UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act (fake-review rules in force from April 2025) and EU consumer law also ban fake reviews.

> **Not legal advice.** These notes summarize obligations in plain language to help teams ask the right questions. Laws change and details matter, so confirm with counsel before relying on them.
